How Satellite Data Works

🛰️

What Is Remote Sensing?

Remote sensing means collecting information about Earth's surface from a distance — usually from satellites or aircraft. Instead of touching the ground, sensors measure reflected sunlight and emitted heat.

🌈

Spectral Bands

Satellites capture light in different wavelengths — visible red, green, blue, plus invisible near-infrared and thermal. Each band reveals different features: water, vegetation, urban areas, and more.

🌿

NDVI in Practice

NDVI = (NIR − Red) / (NIR + Red). Healthy plants strongly reflect near-infrared and absorb red light, giving high NDVI values. Stressed or dead vegetation reflects less NIR, lowering the index.

📐

Spatial Resolution

This is the size of one pixel on the ground. Sentinel-2 gives 10 m pixels — good for fields and forests. Commercial satellites reach 30 cm — enough to count individual cars.

🔄

Temporal Resolution

How often a satellite revisits the same spot. Sentinel-2 returns every 5 days. Geostationary weather satellites image every 10 minutes. More revisits = better change detection.

⚠️

Limits of Satellite Data

Clouds block optical imagery. Resolution limits detail. A single image is a snapshot — ground truth is still needed. And raw data requires processing before it becomes useful information.

About

Satellite Watch

An educational tool that makes real satellite data accessible to everyone — no sign-in, no cost, no setup.

Data Source

Imagery from Esri World Imagery (Maxar, Airbus, Landsat) and NASA GIBS (MODIS). Free for education and non-commercial use.

AI Analysis

Powered by Google Gemini. Sends location coordinates and a prompt — Gemini returns a plain-language description of what the satellite sees.

Privacy

No accounts. No data stored. Location is used only for the current session and never saved or shared.
